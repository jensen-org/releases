# Contributing to Jensen

This is the public home of Jensen. It publishes every build, hosts the download page, and is where
you report bugs and suggest changes. Jensen's source is not published, so there is no source tree
here to patch.

## Reporting a bug

Open a [bug report](https://github.com/jensen-org/releases/issues/new?template=bug.yml). Include the
Jensen version, your platform, and the smallest reproduction you can manage. Remove credentials,
tokens, private source code, and personal data first.

For a security vulnerability, use private reporting rather than a public issue. The
[issue chooser](https://github.com/jensen-org/releases/issues/new/choose) links to it.

## Suggesting a change

Open a [feature request](https://github.com/jensen-org/releases/issues/new?template=feature.yml).

Feedback costs you nothing. Ideas, bug reports, reproduction steps, and design discussion are covered
by the Feedback section of the [Jensen End User License Agreement](LICENSE.md). The copyright holder may
use them freely, and you keep ownership of anything you independently wrote.

## Submitting code

Code, documentation, and configuration contributions carry a licence grant, set out in full in the
Contributions section of [`LICENSE.md`](LICENSE.md). In short: you keep the copyright in your work, and you
grant the copyright holder a perpetual, irrevocable, sublicensable licence to use it under any terms,
including commercial ones. That grant is what lets Jensen ship your patch at all, since Jensen is
licensed commercially and no inbound-equals-outbound assumption applies.

You give that grant by ticking the box in the pull request template. A pull request is not reviewed,
tested, or merged before the box is ticked, because reading an ungranted patch would compromise the
copyright holder's own position on independent development.

By ticking it you confirm that you wrote the work or otherwise have the right to submit it, that your
employer has consented or has no claim to it, and that it contains no confidential, copied, or
incompatible third-party material.

If you would rather not give the grant, open an issue describing the change instead. That is genuinely
useful and is often the faster route.

## Plugins

Jensen's plugin development components are published separately under the MIT license. A plugin you
write is yours. You may publish, sell, and distribute it, provided it does not embed or redistribute
Jensen itself.

## Licensing questions

Using Jensen at work to build products you sell is free and needs no permission. Ask about resale,
redistribution, sublicensing, or hosting through the
[licensing form](https://github.com/jensen-org/releases/issues/new?template=licensing.yml).
